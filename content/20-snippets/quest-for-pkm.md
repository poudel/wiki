systems, or DMS for short. When people say knowledge management
system, they're usually talking about things like obsidian, logseq
that work with plain text, or maybe Notion for something with better
support for media.

Org-roam, obsidian etc. are marketed as note taking or rather personal
knowledge management systems. They both embrace the idea of linking
notes to each other form a graph. The idea is to "build a second
brain", as they say.

## Notes &amp; resources {#notes-and-resources}

Let's define what a note is: A note is something that you authored
yourself. This means that you a quote from someone else is not a note.
A paragraph you copied and pasted is also note a note.

You should keep your audience in mind when authoring a note. In this
context , you are your own audience. Keep this in mind. Phrasing
sentences in certain ways can be helpful but it's not necessary. The
point is to have structure that can be easily parsed by you in the
future.

Notes should always be in a written form. This doesn't mean that it
should always be digital text. No, it can also be handwritten.
Although typing it is the most choice because plain text format has
many inherent benefits.

So what is a resource? Everything that doesn't fit the label "Note" is
a resource.


### Meta notes {#meta-notes}

Meta notes are notes that talk about other notes and resources. You
can use meta notes to create tags and hierarchy e.g. categories.

Meta notes are classified as notes because the user needs to create
and curate them, even if they can refer to resources.


### External links {#external-links}

These are (usually) hyperlinks that refer to external resources. When
you make a link to an external resource, a note is automatically
created for it. This is a "wrapper" note that you can use to add
more information.

## Captured -&gt; Refined -&gt; Curated | Archived {#captured-refined-curated-archived}

The CRC method. Capture, Refine, and Curate. All notes and resources
should go through these steps.


### Captured {#captured}

-   Everything new goes to Inbox.
-   Even when you feel that the thought is refined enough to go into the
    Curated box, resist the temptation to do so.


### Refined {#refined}

-   Go through the inbox
-   Start chronological i.e. the oldest item first
-   This is where you make references to other items.
-   Only make links to curated notes or resources
-   Only the things that you need should go to the Curated box. Things
    that you don't need should


### Curated {#curated}

-   Only put original thought

## Plain text is not enough {#plain-text-is-not-enough}

Say you're discussing with someone at a coffee shop. From that
discussion, you learned something new. You ask your friend to pause
for a minute. Take your phone out, and jot down the idea.

The meeting is over now, it's your turn to pay for the coffee. You do
that, and head back home. On the way back, you remember that you need
to buy some groceries. Inside the store, you see a product package
that gives you an idea about your product. You take your phone out and
make another note.

But now there's a conundrum, you might not be able to easily describe
this image in a written form. Or it might take more than a few words
to write about it and you don't want to spend your afternoon at the
